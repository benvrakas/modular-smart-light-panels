# 0019 — 36 V supply bus

**Date:** 2026-11-22 · **Status:** **Accepted** (user: "switch to 36 volts") · **Supersedes:** the bus voltage in [0010](0010-24v-bus.md) and the 24 V fault numbers in [0011](0011-24v-fault-protection-controller.md) (their circuits stay) · **Changes:** SPEC §2, §3.1–3.4, §5, §8, §9.

## Context

- The seam-bridge springs are the weakest link in power distribution. At 24 V their two +V springs per board carry ≈48 W at the assumed ≈1 A per spring, which is under half of a 100 W Class 2 supply. Their real rating is still unmeasured.
- 48 V was considered and rejected:
  - the TCAN1044 CAN transceiver (±58 V) would no longer survive a CAN-to-bus short with margin;
  - the 60 V parts (TPS54360B buck, F1 PTC, 2N7002) would need 100 V replacements;
  - the SENSE fault resistors would need rework, which would move the edge-code table.
- 36 V keeps almost every 24 V part: the TPS54360B, TCAN1044, F1, 2N7002, the NUP3105 TVS and R_pu = 10 kΩ, so the code table is unaffected.

## Decision: 36 V DC bus

- **Nominal 36 V.** The highest steady voltage allowed is **37.8 V (+5 %)**; don't trim supplies above it. The operating range at a panel is 27–37.8 V, allowing for line drop.
- **Why:**
  - **1.5× the power through the same springs:** ≈72 W per bridge path at ≈1 A per spring. A 100 W supply is 2.8 A.
  - **Lower currents:** a hexagon draws ≈0.12 A (was ≈0.18 A), and I²R loss is 0.44× that at 24 V.
  - **Same panel count per supply:** still ≈23 hexagons per 100 W Class 2 supply. Class 2 allows up to 60 V DC.
- **Supplies:**
  - Kits that are sold ship with a 100 W Class 2 supply.
  - Larger installs, such as the user's own walls, may use a bigger supply, but only through several **fused injection bridges**. Each fuse is sized to what the springs and lanes can carry.
  - 36 V comes from industrial and LED-driver supplies (for example Mean Well's 36 V models). It is also a USB-C PD 3.1 EPR level, which could become an option later, capped at 100 W.
- **Net names:** `+24V` → `+36V` and `+24V_IN` → `+36V_IN` everywhere. The library symbols are renamed `EdgeContacts36V` and `CornerJoint36V`, and the controller netclass `HV24` becomes `HV36`.

## Consequences

- **Thinner margins than at 24 V:** the 60 V parts and the CAN transceiver now sit near the 58.1 V clamp, not 13–15 V below it. They are still within their ratings.
- **Supplies:** 36 V Class 2 supplies are less common than 24 V ones, especially as plug-in adapters.
- **Cost:** about +$0.40 per controller. The 100 V capacitors and the anti-surge resistors are extended parts.
- **Cluster controller (§5):** its buck must be rated above the 58.1 V clamp (≥60 V).
- **Still open:** measure the spring current (gap item 4). If one pair carries ≥1.4 A per spring, a whole 100 W supply fits through one bridge path.
