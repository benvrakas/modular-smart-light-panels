# 0010 — 24 V supply bus

**Date:** 2026-11-16 · **Status:** **Accepted** (user: "I would like to switch to a 24-volt architecture… go ahead with all") · **Changes:** SPEC §3.1, §3.2, §3.3, §3.4, §7.2, §8. Parts in [0003](0003-rgbw-leds-5v-rail.md) (buck, LED-rail switching). · **Superseded (bus voltage) by [0019](0019-36v-bus.md), 2026-11-22:** the bus is now 36 V.

## Context

- The LEDs already run from a per-panel 5 V rail (0003), so the bus voltage only affects distribution.
- On 2026-11-15, 24 V was advised against: the 60 V buck's larger package would not fit the 22 mm active edge board.
- Decision 0009 moves the controller to a rear daughter board, which has room. That objection is gone.

## Decision: 24 V DC bus

Why:
- **Half the current.** A hexagon draws ≈0.18 A instead of ≈0.34 A, and a 25-hexagon domain ≈4.3 A instead of ≈8.5 A.
- **Better spring margin.** The current through the seam-bridge springs halves. Their rating (≈1 A assumed) is still unconfirmed.
- **Narrower lanes.** The thinner lanes help the 10–15 mm edge board target (0009).
- **Less loss.** I²R loss and voltage drop are ¼ for the same power, so runs between injection points can be longer.
- **Same panel count per supply.** The US Class 2 limit is still ≈100 W per supply circuit, so it stays ≈23 hexagons per supply.

Part changes (controller board unless noted):

| Function | V5 (12 V) | V6.0 (24 V) |
|---|---|---|
| Input TVS | SMAJ15A | **SMAJ28A** (C134977): 28 V standoff, ≈45 V clamp |
| 5 V converter | TPS54202 (28 V), enable = LED-rail switch | **TPS54360B** (C524806, 4.5–60 V, 3.5 A, SOIC-8-EP), **always on**. Values per the TI datasheet for 24 → 5 V. |
| LED-rail switch | Buck enable | **P-MOSFET high-side switch** on the LEDs' +5V, gated from PA0 (LED_EN), default off (part to be chosen) |
| CAN transceiver | SN65HVD230 (bus pins −4…16 V) | **TCAN1044VDRQ1** (C1852061): ±58 V bus-fault protection, VCC from +5V, VIO from 3V3 |
| CAN TVS | PESD2CAN (24 V standoff) | A part with a standoff above 24 V (to be chosen) |
| 3.3 V regulator | HT7533 from +12V via 68 Ω | HT7533 from the always-on **+5V**; 68 Ω removed |
| SENSE/DRIVE series resistors | R10 1 kΩ, R11 100 Ω (sized for 12 V faults) | **Re-size and re-rate for 24 V faults.** DRIVE must stay low-ohm for code 0 to read correctly, so it relies on never being adjacent to +24V. |
| Edge boards and bridges | +12V lanes and pads | **+24V** lanes and pads (same positions and rules) |
| Cluster controller | 12 V-input buck | Buck rated ≥45 V input; CAN transceiver with ±58 V fault protection |

Everything connected directly to +24V must be rated above the SMAJ28A's ≈45 V clamp.

## Consequences

- **Cost:** ≈ +$0.75 per panel. Buck +$0.52, CAN transceiver ≈ +$0.20, load switch ≈ +$0.05, TVS ≈ same.
- **Efficiency:** 24 → 5 V conversion is ≈85–88% (12 → 5 V was ≈90%).
- **Supplies:** 24 V Class 2 LED supplies are common and similarly priced.
- **Standby:** the buck stays on, at ≈146 µA quiescent (≈4 mW), for the MCU and CAN wake. The LEDs are fully off through the switch.
